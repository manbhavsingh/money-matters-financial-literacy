# Assessment blueprint

## Intended use

A small formative knowledge check to support learning, not a validated instrument for measuring financial literacy. The questions check basic concepts introduced in the toolkit. Scores should not be used to judge a student's broader ability or personal financial circumstances.

## Learning outcomes and items

| Outcome | Example item | Correct response | Explanation cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan expenses against available money | What is a useful first step in making a budget? | List expected essentials, savings goals, and flexible spending. | A budget makes trade-offs visible before money is spent. |
| Calculate percentage change | A ₹100 item rises by 5%; what is its new price? | ₹105 | ₹100 × 1.05 = ₹105. |
| Explain compounding | How does compound interest differ from simple interest? | Interest may be earned on interest already accumulated. | Compounding is a calculation mechanism, not a guarantee of returns. |
| Evaluate borrowing cost | What should be considered beyond the EMI? | Total repayment, rate, fees, and tenure. | A low EMI can be associated with a longer term and a larger total cost. |
| Recognise a scam signal | What should a learner do with an unsolicited OTP request? | Do not share it; independently verify. | Never share OTPs, PINs, or passwords. |

## Lightweight evaluation plan for future pilots

If this toolkit is used with a real learning group, the educator may conduct a short anonymous pre-activity and post-activity quiz with matched learning objectives. Record the participant count, questions, scoring method, missing responses, and context. Report descriptive change only. A small uncontrolled pilot cannot prove that the toolkit caused any improvement; consider alternative explanations and do not collect unnecessary personal data.
