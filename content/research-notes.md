# Research and content notes

## Scope

Money Matters is an independent educational prototype for students, approximately ages 14–18. It focuses on core concepts: needs and wants, budgeting, simple and compound interest, inflation, borrowing, and basic fraud awareness. It does not recommend financial products or ask learners to disclose their actual financial circumstances.

## Design rationale

1. **Concrete examples before abstraction.** Fictional student scenarios introduce the question before a formula.
2. **Worked examples and practice.** Each lesson includes a calculation or decision task.
3. **Formative feedback.** The quiz explains the reasoning, not just the correct option.
4. **Transparent assumptions.** Calculators expose their assumptions and limitations.
5. **Source traceability.** Public educational resources are linked so users can verify foundational concepts.

## Source notes

- Reserve Bank of India, *Financial Literacy for School Children*: needs and wants, budgeting, income/expenses, borrowing. PDF: https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/English/content/01SCHOOL20042018.pdf
- Reserve Bank of India, *Financial Awareness Messages (FAME)*: inflation, interest, responsible money practices. PDF: https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/images/FAME%20Booklet%20second%20edition/FAME%20Booklet%20English/FAME30072020.pdf
- Reserve Bank of India, *National Strategy for Financial Education 2020–2025*: financial knowledge, attitude, behaviour, and audience-specific educational approaches. PDF: https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/Content/PDFs/English_16042021.pdf
- Reserve Bank of India, *Annual Report 2024–25*, chapter on credit delivery and financial inclusion: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1434

These sources are background references, not endorsements of this project. Content is paraphrased and original classroom examples have been created for the toolkit. Users should consult the primary sources for context and latest updates.

## Limitations

- The material is a prototype; it has not been tested in a classroom or validated by a financial-education specialist.
- The calculators use simplified mathematical assumptions and do not account for all real-world product conditions.
- Inflation examples assume a constant rate for illustration. Actual inflation varies and price indices use defined baskets and methods.
- Learning outcomes have not been measured. Any future pilot should transparently document the method and avoid causal claims from small uncontrolled samples.
