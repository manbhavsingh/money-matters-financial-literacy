## Activity 1 — Build a budget

A fictional student has ₹2,500 for the month. Their plan includes travel (₹700), meals and snacks (₹650), phone/data (₹300), school supplies (₹250), entertainment (₹350), and savings (₹300).

1. Add the planned amounts. Total: ₹__________
2. Does the plan fit within ₹2,500? __________
3. How much is left or overspent? ₹__________
4. What questions would you ask before suggesting a change to the plan?

## Activity 2 — Interest over time

A deposit of ₹2,000 earns 4% annual simple interest for 3 years. Assume no fees or withdrawals.

1. Write the simple-interest formula: ____________________
2. Calculate the interest: ₹__________
3. Calculate the amount at the end: ₹__________
4. Why should you read the conditions of a real savings product rather than rely only on this example?

## Activity 3 — A change in prices

A bus pass costs ₹500. In a hypothetical scenario, the price rises by 6%.

1. What is 6% of ₹500? ₹__________
2. What is the new price? ₹__________
3. Does this one price change tell us the overall inflation rate? Why or why not?

## Activity 4 — Think before borrowing

## Activity 5 — Red-flag check

A stranger messages you promising guaranteed high returns and asks you to share an OTP.

- What are the warning signs?
- What should you do next?
- Where could you verify the claim independently?

# Answer guide (for facilitators)

**Activity 1:** Total = ₹2,550; the plan exceeds the stated amount by ₹50. Discuss context and trade-offs rather than assuming which expense should be cut.

**Activity 2:** Simple interest = P × r × t = ₹2,000 × 0.04 × 3 = ₹240. Total = ₹2,240. Real products have terms, risks, eligibility, fees, and compounding rules.

**Activity 3:** 6% of ₹500 = ₹30; new price = ₹530. No. Inflation is measured using a wider basket of goods/services and a defined method.

**Activity 4:** Possible answers: principal, interest rate, APR/annualised cost if available, EMI, total repayment, tenure, processing fees, penalties, rate type, and contract terms.

**Activity 5:** Guaranteed high returns, unsolicited message, and OTP request are warning signs. Do not share credentials or transfer money; verify through official details obtained independently and ask a trusted person/institution for help.
